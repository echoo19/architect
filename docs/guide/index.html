Architect

How to use it

Contents

How to read this guide

Each chapter says what you will do, gives the steps, shows what you will see, and says what to do if something goes wrong. Words in bold are words on the screen. Commands and file names are in a typewriter font. The figures are the app's own screens.

You describe a job. A large model teaches a small one to do it, and the small one stays on your computer as your agent.

Two models

Architect uses two models from Meta. Spark, the teacher, is large. You reach it over the internet with a key. Glimmer, the student, is small. It runs on your computer through Ollama.

What you do

You describe a job and attach the files it works from. Spark reads them and asks about anything they leave open. Then it drafts the job. If your files include a policy with prices or rules, Spark turns it into a rule that you check and confirm.

What Spark does

Spark watches Glimmer try the job. When Glimmer gets something wrong, Spark finds the step where it went wrong and edits Glimmer's harness: its instructions, the notes it reads and small tools. A change stays only if Glimmer does better on check requests that Spark never sees. Spark sees the score and nothing else about them.

The sealed test

When training ends, Glimmer takes a final test on its own, with Spark disconnected. The requests in it were put aside before training started. Spark never sees them. If Glimmer passes, it graduates and becomes your agent.

Your agent

The agent runs offline on your computer. It works on your files and asks before it changes one. It talks to nothing except the model on your computer. Later you can teach it more, keep versions, share it, and run it with the architect command.

Check four things first: your computer, Ollama, a key for the teacher, and what teaching will cost.

Your computer

Architect runs on Windows and macOS. Glimmer runs on the graphics card, so the size of its memory decides which build of Glimmer you can use.

On Windows, Architect reads the memory of an NVIDIA card. On a Mac with Apple silicon, the graphics chip shares the computer's memory, and Architect counts about 70 percent of it. On other hardware it can't tell, and it recommends the larger build.

Build Download On disk Graphics memory
Glimmer, recommended. Most accurate. 17 GB 17 GB 20 GB or more
Glimmer, smaller. Lighter, but less accurate. 14 GB 10 GB 11 GB or more. Made for a 12 GB card.

If a build doesn't fit, part of it runs on the processor. It still works, but it is slower. The first setup screen says so: Part runs on the processor, so it is slower.

Ollama

Glimmer runs through Ollama, a free app from ollama.com. Install it and open it before you start. Architect checks that it is running.

A key for the teacher

Spark needs a key from Meta. The setup screen has a button for it, Get one from Meta.

You can choose another teacher instead. Sign in to OpenRouter and pick any of its models. Use a key from OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini or another service. Or use a model on your own computer, in Ollama or LM Studio. A teacher on your computer is free, and nothing you send leaves your computer.

Keys are stored encrypted on your computer.

Two optional accounts

An OpenRouter account lets Glimmer practice on an online copy of itself, which is about ten times faster. A Hugging Face token is needed to publish an agent. It is also needed if you let Spark retrain Glimmer's weights.

What teaching costs

Part Cost
Glimmer on your computer: practice, the final test, chats Free
Spark, the teacher Billed by its provider for the text it reads and writes. Meta lists Spark 1.3 at $1.25 per million tokens read and $4.25 per million written.
Online practice, if you choose it Billed by the online host. The Define screen shows an estimate.

Before training starts, the Define screen shows an estimated time and cost, and a spending cap for Spark. A job you describe starts with a $5 cap, and you can change it. Training stops before it goes over. In a returns desk run the Architect team measured, teaching cost 42 cents of Spark.

Settings, under Training, has How hard Spark thinks. More thinking can find subtler fixes but costs more per round.

Time matters more than money. On your computer Glimmer takes about 2.5 minutes a task, so rounds are slow and the final test of 60 tasks takes about 2.5 hours. A job starts with a time limit of 4 hours. If not even one round fits, Architect says so and offers more time or online practice (chapters 6 and 7).

Four screens set Architect up. They take a few minutes. The model downloads while you carry on.

Opening it

The app isn't signed yet, so your system may warn you the first time. On Windows, choose More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, right-click the app and choose Open.

Steps

The steps are named at the top of the window: Model, Teacher, Faster training and Done.

  1. Model. Architect picks the build of Glimmer that suits your graphics card and marks it Recommended. Press Download Glimmer. The download is large and continues while you finish setup. You can choose the smaller build, press Download later, or open Use a different model to pick a model that is already on your computer. See .

  2. Teacher. Paste your Meta key into Meta API key and press Test connection. A message says Connected when it works. Other ways to connect a teacher shows the other choices: OpenRouter, Another service and On this computer. See .

  3. Faster training. Optional. Sign in to OpenRouter, or use a key, so Glimmer can practice on an online copy of itself. The finished agent still runs only on your computer. Press Skip if you don't want it.

  4. Done. The last screen says You're set up and shows the state of Glimmer and Spark. Press Get started.

What you will see

The home screen. With no agents yet it says No agents yet and offers three buttons: New agent, Try the sample job and Try a ready-made agent. The sample job is a small job that Architect trains an agent for while you watch. The ready-made agent is already trained, so you can start using it at once.

  3. Press the paperclip and pick the files the job works from. You can pick several at once.

Files that work

Type Use it for
.csv Records the agent looks up and works out, such as invoices, jobs or tickets. Architect makes practice tasks from the rows.
.txt, .md A policy, a rate card or rules. A text file is read whole, up to 30,000 characters, and 60,000 across all text files. Attach just the part the job needs.
.jsonl Optional. Example tasks with their answers, one task per line. Architect uses them as they are.

You need at least 30 rows of records in all, or 30 example tasks. You can attach up to 12 files, 25 MB each and 60 MB together.

Not yet supported: spreadsheets such as .xlsx, PDFs, Word documents and JSON files. Save or export them as CSV or text first.

Writing the description

  • Say what the agent should do, as tasks. "Price the visits the technicians finished" is better than "help with billing".
  • Say what each file holds if its name doesn't.
  • Say what it must not do. Spark lists this under What it won't do.
  • Say how good it has to be if you have a target. If you don't, Spark sets one and you can change it (chapter 6).
  • Put prices and rules in a file, not only in the description. Architect turns the file into a rule you can check.

A good description

Close out the day for our home-services office: price the visits the technicians finished, find customers with overdue balances, and book the earliest technician for urgent calls.

It names three tasks. The files fit them: jobs.csv, invoices.csv, technicians.csv, bookings.csv, policy.md and tasks.jsonl.

A weak description

Help me with my business.

It names no task and no limits, and it has no files. Spark has nothing to make practice tasks from, and nothing says what a right answer looks like.

Spark asks only what your files leave open. Your answers change what the agent does and how its answers are checked.

Steps

  1. Read the question. Spark asks one at a time and offers two to four short answers as buttons.

  2. Press the answer that is true for your business. If none fits, type your own in the Reply box.

  3. Spark asks at most three questions. At any point you can choose Draft it now to make it use what it has.

  4. When it has enough, it says That's everything I need. I've drafted the job for you to review. Press Review the job. See .

What you will see

Your message and files appear first, then Spark's reply. Some questions come from Architect itself. If your records have empty cells, or marks such as N/A or a dash where a number belongs, Architect asks what to do with them: Count it as zero or Leave those rows out.

Those answers change the numbers the agent is tested on, so they need a real answer. Draft it now does not stand in for one.

Answering well

  • Answer for what happens in your business, not for what sounds right. If you are unsure, pick the safer option.
  • Short answers are fine.
  • If you notice your description was wrong, press Edit the description. Your words and files come back as you left them.

  3. If the screen has Check the rule, do that next (below).

  4. Choose where to train (below).

Check the rule

If your files include a policy with prices, discounts or rounding, Spark turns it into a rule. Architect works out the right answer to every task from that rule and grades Glimmer against those answers. If the rule is wrong, the grades are wrong too. So nothing trains until you confirm it. See .

  1. Under From your policy, read the lines Spark took. Each shows where it came from: a file, or your answer. Are they the right lines?

  2. Under The rule, in words, read the numbered steps. Is that how your business works?

  3. Under Examples, pick one and work it out yourself. Every example shows each step. An example tagged Not from your records was made up to show how the rule rounds.

  4. Read the notes at the bottom. They say how many rows were left out of the tasks, and why.

  5. If all is right, press Confirm the rule. If not, press Change the description. Your words and files come back as you left them, and Spark may ask its questions again.

Start training stays off, and the bar says Confirm the rule first, until you confirm. If your records change afterwards, a badge says Changed since you confirmed it. Press Describe the job again so the tasks are made from the records as they are now.

The Results screen later repeats the point: the score shows how often the agent agreed with the rule you confirmed. It can't show whether the rule is right for your business. Only you can check that.

Spark teaches Glimmer in rounds. You can watch, answer a question, pause or stop. Every limit is a hard cap.

Steps

  1. Press Start training. Keep Architect open while it trains.

  2. Watch the Train screen. The large panel shows Spark and Glimmer and what each is doing. See .

  3. If Spark has a question, answer it in the round panel (below).

  4. When training ends, Glimmer takes the final test by itself. Then press See the results. If you are at the top of the page, the app opens the results for you.

What a round is

Before round 1, Architect scores Glimmer on the check tasks, so there is a starting score. If the job starts blank, round 1 is a first draft: Spark writes a whole set of instructions from the job and the example tasks. After that, each round has four steps.

  1. Practice. Glimmer works through a set of practice tasks.

  2. Diagnose. Spark reads the failures and finds the step where Glimmer went wrong. It picks one change and says what it expects, for example +3 to +6 check tasks.

  3. Change. Architect applies the change to a copy of the harness. Spark can change the instructions and steps, what Glimmer reads and when, and small tools. It can also ask you a question. Retraining Glimmer's weights can't run without a Hugging Face key and a training budget.

  4. Score. Glimmer takes the check tasks again with the change.

The check tasks are separate from the practice tasks. Spark sees only their score.

Kept or undone

The Rounds list shows how each change ended.

Outcome What it means
Kept It gained enough on the check tasks and broke no rule. The agent keeps it.
No gain It didn't gain enough, so it was undone.
Made it worse It made the check tasks worse or broke a rule, so it was undone.
Couldn't be tried It couldn't be tried in this run, so nothing changed.
Stopped Training ended before it was scored. The agent never took it.

For a job you describe, a change must gain at least 2 check tasks, and no task type may lose more than 1. A promising change is measured twice, to rule out luck. You can change these numbers in Advanced settings, under When a change stays. See .

The rest of the screen

  • The score chart shows the share of check tasks right after each round, with the target as a dotted line.
  • Time, Spark's cost and Rounds show how much of each limit is used. Changes kept counts the changes kept out of those tried.
  • Worked examples lists corrected examples, each marked Checker passed it or Checker failed it. It says No worked examples yet until there are some.
  • Rounds has a row for each change. Open a row to see the diagnosis and the scores before and after.
  • Latest attempts lists Glimmer's attempts. Open one to step through what it did.

When Spark asks you

Sometimes Spark stops to ask one question. The run pauses, the sidebar row says Needs you, and the round panel shows the question. Type your answer in Your answer and press Send answer. Spark keeps it as what you meant and sees it in every round after.

Tick This changes what counts as success only if your answer really does. The run then stops, because the final test belongs to the job as it was. You would train again on the changed job.

Limits

Rounds, Spark's dollars and time are hard caps. Architect checks them before every call to Spark and before every round. A call that would go over a cap does not start. A job you describe starts with 8 rounds, $5 and 4 hours. Paused time doesn't count. Practice still running when the time is up is cut off and not scored, and the run goes on to its final test.

Training ends when it reaches the target, uses a limit, or stops finding gains. The Rounds list closes with the reason, such as Reached the target, Used all 8 rounds, Spark's budget ran out, The time limit ran out or No gain in 4 rounds in a row.

Pause and stop

Pause holds training. Resume carries on from where it stopped. Stop ends training after the step in progress. If a change has been kept, the box asks Stop training? and offers Stop and run the final test: the agent keeps its best version so far and Glimmer takes the final test without Spark. If no change has been kept, a stop makes no agent.

You can leave the screen. Training goes on, and the sidebar lists the job as Training, Paused, Needs you or Results ready. Architect saves every step. If it closes, open it again and find the job in the sidebar.

The rest of the page

Chat with your agent, let it work on your files, and approve each change. It works offline.

Steps

  1. Press Start using on the Results screen. A chat opens. Next time, pick the agent in the sidebar.

  2. Ask in plain words in the box that says Ask and the agent's name. A new chat offers a few requests to start with, taken from what the agent passed on its test.

  3. Watch the reply. The line above it, such as Worked for 2 min 21 s, opens to show each step: what the agent read, searched or ran. See .

  4. If it wants to change a file, it shows the change first (below).

  5. The plus beside the agent's name starts a new chat. Each chat keeps its own history.

Files and folders

The agent sees only the folders you gave it, and only the files its job names. Press the three dots beside the agent's name, then Permissions. Each folder shows the place on your computer it points to, with a button to choose another. Changing one runs this computer's checks again.

Approving changes

Saving files sits under the message box. A new agent starts on Ask first. When the agent wants to change a file, a card shows the file and the rows or lines it would write, and the reply waits: Waiting for your approval. In the sidebar, a chat that waits shows Approve. See .

Button What it does
Allow once Saves this change.
Deny Does not save it.
Allow saving from now on Saves this change and later ones without asking. It sets Saving files to Allow. Each change still shows in the chat.

Set Saving files to Never if the agent should only read.

Working offline

The pill at the top says Running on this computer. The agent talks only to the model on your computer. You can turn off the internet and keep using it. The first reply may be slow while the model loads. If another job is using the graphics card, the step says Waiting: your computer is busy with another job. and goes on when the card is free.

What it refuses

An agent does its job and nothing else. Ask for something outside it and it says so, with the reason, instead of guessing. What it leaves to you is listed on the Results screen under What it won't do.

If something goes wrong

If you see Do this
A slow reply Glimmer is slow on a computer without much graphics memory. Architect plans for about 2.5 minutes a task. A shorter request helps.
Not saved on a change you allowed The card says why. Press Try again, or Don't save.
Not applied You stopped the reply before answering, so the change was not made.

Can't run on this computer Press the three dots beside the agent's name, then General. Works on this computer says what failed and offers a fix.

When the agent gets something wrong, say so. You choose what happens next, and the version in use never changes by itself.

Teach this

  1. Under the wrong reply, press the flag, Flag for teaching. A panel called Teach this opens. See .

  2. In What was wrong, say what it got wrong. Two more boxes are optional: What it should have done and What it should have used, where you pick files or paste rows.

  3. Read the line at the bottom. Only this exchange, its steps and your notes go to Spark, for up to a small cost. Nothing goes until you press Share with Spark.

  4. Spark proposes a lesson. It says what kind it is, what change it would make, whether practice is needed, and what reading it cost. See .

Kinds of lesson

Kind What happens
Rule You state something it should do every time. It is added to the agent's instructions. No practice is needed.
Missing fact It lacked a fact or a file. This is added to its instructions too.
Needs practice It keeps making the same kind of mistake. Improve practices tasks like it.
Unclear request Nothing changes until you say which you meant.
Can't be checked No change.

For a rule or a missing fact, Save as version and its number adds the lesson to the agent as a new version at once. That version isn't used until you choose it, and it hasn't taken a final test. Save for Improve keeps the lesson with your other flagged answers. Discard throws it away. Say more and send again lets you add detail.

Improve

Improve teaches your saved lessons together. Press the three dots beside the agent's name, then Teaching. Under Lessons, Flagged answers lists what you flagged, and Improve starts the run.

  1. Press Improve. A box asks Improve and the agent's name, and says what will happen: it teaches your lessons to a copy of the current version, saves a new version only if it scores better on the check tasks, and stops at the caps it shows. The current version keeps answering meanwhile.

  2. Press Start improving. The Train screen opens, with the steps Lessons, Improve and Results. You can pause or stop it.

  3. When it ends, press See what changed. The screen shows the score before and after on the check tasks. A new version is marked Not in use yet, and it hasn't taken a final test.

Versions

Versions, under Teaching, lists every version of the agent, newest first. Each is In use, New, not in use yet, Earlier version or Set aside, and shows its final test result, No final test yet, or how far it fell short of its target. See .

  • To use a new version, press Use this version. A box says what changes: new replies use that version's setup and trained weights, and the version you leave stays.
  • To go back, press Go back to this version on an earlier one. It restores that version's setup and weights together.
  • A version that came from another computer never writes without asking. Saving files goes back to Ask first when you use it.

If something goes wrong

If you see Do this
Can't be taught on this computer The row says why. An agent that came from another computer may need its model or runtime set up first.
Not shared The exchange matches a question from the final test, which Spark must never see. Flag a different answer.
Too long to review Reading it would cost more than the limit. Shorten your note, or flag a shorter answer.
No new version No change scored better on the check tasks. The current version stays, and the lessons go back in the queue.

Export an agent as one file, publish it to Hugging Face, or install one that someone sent you.

Export a package file

  1. Press the three dots beside the agent's name, then Export. Choose Package file. See .

  2. Read In the package: the setup, the model's name, the job and the test results, plus trained weights if it has them. The file is named like service-desk-v2.architect.zip.

  3. Read Stays on this computer: your folder choices and model settings, your API keys, and the attempts and notes from training. Chats stay too.

  4. A folder's files go only if you switch them on, with the switch beside Files in data/.

  5. Read Scan. It looks for lines that look like a key or a private path. If it finds some, read each, then tick Export anyway.

  6. Press Export and choose a folder. Export one part saves only the setup, the trained weights or the results.

The scan is a best effort. Trained weights may keep traces of the data they learned from, and a setup written from your tasks may quote them. Leaving files out doesn't make an agent anonymous.

Publish to Hugging Face

  1. In the same tab, choose Hugging Face. See .

  2. Type the repository as owner/name. Choose Private, which is the default and lets only accounts you allow see it, or Public, which lets anyone see and download it.

  3. Check Token. It comes from Settings (Models, then Hugging Face), from HF_TOKEN on your computer, or from hf auth login. It goes only to Hugging Face's own service, never into the package.

  4. Read Files, press Preview to read the model card, and read Scan.

  5. Press Publish version and its number. The panel shows progress, then the repository and its commit, with the command that installs exactly that commit.

An existing repository keeps its visibility: a private one stays private. If a publication fails, it says Didn't finish. Press Finish publishing to complete the same one. The agent still runs with Architect on the computer that installs it, not on Hugging Face.

Import

  1. Press the three dots beside New agent in the sidebar. Choose Import agent for a file or folder, or Install from Hugging Face and type owner/name. A commit is optional.

  2. Package. Architect checks the package without running any of it and says Safe to open. It shows whether your computer can run it and has the model, and how it did for its author, labelled Tested by its author. See .

  3. Folders. Where are its files? Choose a folder on your computer for each folder the agent works in.

  4. Permissions. What may it do? For each folder choose Not allowed, Read or Read and change, never more than the package asks for. Internet access reads Never, by design.

  5. Install. Architect installs it and runs its checks. When they pass, press Start a chat.

A package asks for access, and nothing is granted unless you grant it. The author's score is not a test on your files. The checks on your computer show that the agent runs here. They are not a new final test. A package that is a new version of an agent you already have joins as a version and waits until you choose to use it.

The architect command lists, installs, runs, checks, exports and publishes agents from a terminal, without opening the app.

What it is

It works on the same agents as the app, in the same folder, with the same rules. An agent sees only the folders you gave it, asks before it changes a file unless you allow it, and runs only on a model on your computer. The teacher is never contacted. Only publish, and install from Hugging Face, go online, and only to Hugging Face.

Getting it

The command comes with the installer.

Windows architect.cmd, beside Architect.exe in the folder the app installed to (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Architect by default). Add that folder to PATH to type architect anywhere.
macOS Architect.app/Contents/Resources/architect. Link it into a folder on your PATH. 
ln -s /Applications/Architect.app/Contents/Resources/architect \
  /usr/local/bin/architect

Commands

Command What it does
architect list Lists every agent on this computer, with its name, id, active version and whether it can run here.
architect install Installs a package, or a version published to Hugging Face. Everything is checked before any of its code loads.
architect run Talks to an agent, or runs one task from a file.
architect inspect Shows an agent's versions, model, folders, permissions, the author's results and this computer's checks. Given a package file, it checks the package without installing it.
architect verify Runs the active version's installation checks again. It is not an evaluation.
architect export Writes a version as one package file, after the same review the app shows.
architect publish Publishes a version to a Hugging Face repository, private unless you add --public. --dry-run shows what would go and sends nothing.

An agent is named by the name architect list shows, or by its id. Add --json to list, install, inspect, verify, export and publish for output a script can read. 

architect install ./desk.architect.zip --bind data=./files
architect install hf://jake/service-desk --revision 3f1c9e2a
architect export service-desk --output ./desk.architect.zip
architect publish service-desk --repo jake/service-desk --private

A package asks for access and nothing is granted that the command doesn't name. --bind points one of the package's folders at a folder here, and --grant gives it read, read_write or none. --writes takes ask (the default), allow or never.

Talking to an agent

architect run service-desk starts a conversation. Type one request per line. The answer streams to the terminal and the steps go to stderr. A change that needs approval is asked about in the terminal. Ctrl+C stops a reply, and Ctrl+D ends the conversation. 

$ architect run service-desk
> Who owes us money?
  read data/invoices.csv (3 rows)
Ruiz, Ben owes $45.50 on INV-2, due November 15.

Running one task from a script

Give run a request as a JSON file, and it writes the result as JSON. Results go to stdout and everything else to stderr, so a script can read stdout. 

architect run service-desk --input request.json --output result.json --non-interactive
{
  "format": "architect.run-request/1",
  "task": "Who owes us money, and how much?",
  "inputs": ["data/invoices.csv"],
  "version": 2
}

task is what to do, in the words you would use in the app. inputs and version are optional. --non-interactive means nobody can be asked, so a change that needs approval ends the run with the error approval_required, and nothing is written. The result has a status, an exit_code, the answer, the steps taken, and an error with a code and a hint when something went wrong.

Exit codes

Code Outcome When
0 succeeded It did what was asked.
1 runtime_error Something failed while running: the model runtime isn't there, a time limit ran out, or Hugging Face couldn't be reached.
2 invalid_input The command line or the request is malformed, or names something that isn't there.
3 not_permitted A requirement or permission isn't met: a change that needs approval, a folder that isn't bound or granted, a model that isn't on this computer, or a missing token.
4 task_failed The agent ran and didn't do the task. It refused it, or stopped without an answer.
130 cancelled Ctrl+C or a stop signal ended it.

If something goes wrong

If you see Do this
Exit 3 after install A folder the package needs is not bound or granted. The checks say which. Run again with --bind and --grant.
Exit 1, and the model isn't answering Start Ollama. architect verify checks the agent again.
The command waits, then exits 1 The app is using the graphics card. The command waits up to 5 minutes, then says what holds it.
Exit 3 after export or publish, with a list The scan found lines that look like a key or a private path. Check them. If they are fine, run again with --accept-findings and the id it printed.

Most of Architect stays on your computer. This chapter says exactly what doesn't, and what never does.

What can leave

When What goes To whom
You describe a job Your description and the files you attach. A text file goes whole. For a table, Spark sees its columns, examples of what is in them, and the first five rows. Your teacher. Nothing leaves if the teacher runs on your computer.
It trains Practice attempts and samples of your files, so Spark can diagnose failures. For the check tasks, only the score. Your teacher.
Practice online, if you choose it Tasks and files. The online host you chose.
You use Teach this After you confirm, the one exchange you flag, its steps and your notes, for a capped cost. Your teacher.
You download a model The download. Ollama, or Hugging Face.
You publish or install from Hugging Face The package you choose. Your token goes only to Hugging Face's own service. Hugging Face.

Each teacher has its own data terms. For Spark, the app says: Meta doesn't train on what you send. Read your provider's terms if you use another teacher. Settings, under Privacy, gives the short version.

What never leaves

  • The final test. Spark never sees its requests, its answers or its scores before the agent graduates. Architect checks everything it sends to Spark against the final test, and refuses to send a match. Nothing about the final test goes back into training.
  • Your chats. Spark never sees them. The one exception is Teach this, and only after you press Share with Spark.
  • Your agent's work. A finished agent talks only to the model on your computer. Architect refuses any other connection, and an agent never calls Spark.
  • Usage data. Architect collects none.
  • Your keys. They are stored encrypted on your computer. They never go into a package.

What Spark can and can't change

Spark can change an agent's harness: its instructions, what it reads and when, and small tools. It can't change how answers are graded, the task sets, the final test, or what the agent may access.

The small tools Spark writes run in a sandbox. They have no network and no clock, and they can read only the files the job names, without changing them. A change to your files needs your approval unless you set Saving files to Allow.

Rounds, Spark's dollars and time are hard caps. A call that would go over one does not start.

If you want to share less

  • Use a teacher that runs on your computer, in Ollama or LM Studio. Then nothing leaves.
  • Attach only the files the job needs. For a policy, attach only the section it needs.
  • Choose Train on this computer on the Define screen instead of Train online.
  • Under each choice, the app says what leaves. Read it before you press the button.

The words this guide and the app use, in the order you will meet them.

Agent
What Architect makes for a job: Glimmer with its harness. It runs offline on your computer.
Spark
The teacher. A large model, Muse Spark by default, reached over the internet with a key.
Glimmer
The student. A small model, Muse Glimmer, that runs on your computer through Ollama.
Ollama
A free app that runs models on your computer. Glimmer runs through it.
Job
What you describe: the tasks, the files they use, the limits and the target.
Harness
The plain folder of files that tells Glimmer how to do the job: instructions, notes it reads, and small tools. Spark edits it.
Rule
A policy with prices or rounding that Spark turned into exact steps. You confirm it before anything trains.
Round
One cycle of training: practice, diagnose, change, score.
Practice tasks
Tasks Glimmer works through so Spark can see where it goes wrong.
Check tasks
Tasks Spark never sees. Spark gets only their score, and the score decides which changes stay.
Final test
Tasks put aside before training and opened once, at the end, with Spark disconnected.
Kept, undone
A change is kept if it gains enough on the check tasks and breaks no rule. Otherwise it is undone.
Graduated
Passed the final test on this computer. The agent is marked Ready.
Version
A saved state of an agent. A new version waits until you choose to use it, and an earlier one can be restored.
Teach this
The flag under a reply. It turns a wrong answer into a lesson.
Improve
A training run that teaches the saved lessons and makes a new version if it scores better.
Weights
The numbers inside a model. Retraining them is an option for Spark, and it needs a Hugging Face key and a training budget.
Package
An agent exported as one .architect.zip file.
Hugging Face
A site where models are shared. Architect can publish an agent to it and install one from it.
